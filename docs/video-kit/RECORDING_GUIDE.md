# Recording guide

Two separate videos are planned, approximately 60 seconds each. These files are scripts and shot lists, not rendered video files.

## Prepare

1. Start the local service with `make demo` (or use the already running instance).
2. Open http://127.0.0.1:8765/ and close unrelated tabs/windows in the recording area.
3. Click Reset. Rehearse the sequence in DEMO_VIDEO.md without recording.
4. For the technical explainer, have docs/ARCHITECTURE.md and reports/evaluation.md ready. Use the diagram and visible evidence rather than scrolling through source code.
5. Record in landscape, with text large enough to read. Keep essential simulator/research labels visible.

## Capture on this Mac

Press Shift–Command–5 to open macOS screen capture. Choose the recording area and, if you want live narration, select your microphone in Options. Start recording and follow the shot list. Stop using the recording control. The output is a screen recording; record voice separately if that produces clearer delivery.

Trim to 60 seconds per video and add captions matching the narration. Use an editor you already have; no paid tool is required by this plan. Rehearsal is necessary—word count alone cannot guarantee an exact runtime. Export using the submission platform's required format, size and aspect ratio once those requirements are known.

## Assistance available

The assistant can revise narration, shorten it for your speaking speed, translate captions, prepare presentation material, and review an attached recording against the actual implementation. Video rendering/export depends on the available media tools and supplied recordings. No finished video is claimed in this package.

## Final review

- Actual on-screen replies match the narration.
- No claim of real SMS or an officer receiving a message.
- No claim that the classifier predicts crop success or that farmer impact has been measured.
- English model weakness and pending expert review appear in the technical video.
- No account details, unrelated notifications or private user information are visible.
- The repository is private; give judges authorized access if their submission process requires it.
